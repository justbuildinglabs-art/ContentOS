# Creator template

This is the shape of `.contentos/creator.md`. `setup` writes the short
answers a creator gives it under these headings and leaves the rest for the
creator to fill in by hand. Every other stage reads this file as ground
truth: a claim, a number, or a word that is not written here does not exist
for the writer or the reviewer. Keep each answer short and concrete. Plain
language, no em dashes.

## Creator
Say your name or handle and what you make, in words a stranger would
understand on the first read.

## One-liner
One sentence that explains what you do to someone who has never heard of you.

## Pillars
List the 3 to 5 topics you post about.

## Audience profile
Describe the one viewer this is for, specific enough that a writer could
picture them walking into a room.

- Who specifically: a real kind of person, not a demographic slice.
- Their number one frustration or want, in their own words: how they would actually say it, not how a brand would.
- What they already watch and why it falls short: the other creators, accounts, or content that did not fully deliver.
- The fear behind it: what they are actually afraid of if nothing changes.
- The outcome they want: the specific result, not a vague feeling.
- Top 3 objections: the reasons they would not act even if they wanted to.
- The proof that flips them: what fact or demo actually moves this person.

## What you promote
Optional. Name what you promote, if anything, so a script can end on it
instead of a plain follow ask. Leave this blank if you have nothing to
promote yet.

- What it is: the app, product, service, or offer, in one line.
- The objection that stops people: the reason someone would not take you up on it.

## Payoff moments
Name the specific on-screen moments a script can show: a result, a screen,
a before and after, a tool doing the thing. When you promote something,
include the moments that show it working.

## Allowed claims
List every claim, number, feature, or result the writer may state as fact,
about you, the tools and topics you cover, or what you promote.

## Forbidden claims
List what you must never claim: medical outcomes, income promises, legal
guarantees, or anything you are not ready to defend to a regulator.

## Proof assets
List the testimonials, reviews, screenshots, numbers, or results that
genuinely exist and may be shown on screen.

## Brand voice
Describe how you sound when you talk, specific enough that two different
writers would land on the same voice.

- 3 adjectives it is: the words that describe the voice at its best.
- 3 adjectives it is not: the nearest wrong turns, named so a writer avoids them.
- Sentence rules: how long a sentence runs, contractions or not, first or second person.
- 10 on-brand words: words this voice actually reaches for.
- 10 off-limits words: words that would make you wince if the writer used them.
- 3 sample sentences: real sentences in the voice, ready to imitate.

## CTA
Give the one action a viewer should take, and the objection that stops most
people from taking it. Without an offer, the default ask is a follow,
comment, save, or share.

## Hashtag seeds
List 10 to 20 hashtags the writer can mix into a caption: some broad, some
narrow to your exact niche.

## Competitors
List the 3 to 8 Instagram accounts in your niche that research scrapes for
outliers, by handle.

## Format accounts
Optional. List up to 5 Instagram accounts, from any niche, whose formats
travel well and are worth adapting into your own, by handle.
