# Patterns

## Proven hooks

1. Curiosity gap on a number. DWN006 opens with "We were NOT ready for this one" and pays it off with a streak count.
2. Bold claim with a checkable number. HAB005 leads with a view count and spends the rest of the video on the cause.
3. Story open on a surprise. SPA006 says the hack was not meant to blow up, which buys the first three seconds.
4. Pain call out on a moment. DWN003 names the day four quit, and the comments agree with it.
5. Question that is easy to answer. DWN001 asks viewers to rate a board, so the comment writes itself.

## Recurring formats

Screen demos and talking heads carry the two strongest reels here. DWN006 is a plain screen recording. HAB005 is one person to camera with the phone in shot. SPA006 shows that a trend remix still works when the middle of the video holds a real technique. Slideshow text (DWN001) and voiceover b roll (DWN003) are the cheapest to produce and still land.

## Saturated angles to avoid

The five productivity hacks you need listicle. The aesthetic montage set to a trending sound with no technique in it. The talking head who says consistency is key and never shows a screen. The rate my setup post built on a board nobody actually uses. Every one of these is already in the feed.

## Structural recommendation

Aim for 17 to 27 seconds. Put the promise in the first line and the screen on camera by second four. Three or four beats is enough. Use your own audio when you can speak to the claim, and keep trending sounds for reach only.
